A locked-off camera films this painted cyberpunk skyline for five seconds. The camera never moves — no pan, tilt, zoom, dolly or perspective change — and the painting itself is not redrawn: every building, the suspension bridge, the shoreline, the billboard artwork and the cloud banks keep their exact shapes and positions for the whole clip. The clouds may stay where they are.

What moves is the city's light. Window lights flicker, and lit windows switch off while dark ones switch on, all over the towers. The neon signs and the big illuminated billboards pulse and shift in brightness. Aerial vehicles travel along their lanes between the towers as small moving points of red and cyan light. Red beacons blink on the tallest masts and antennae. The reflections on the water ripple and shimmer continuously.

Nothing is added or removed: no new buildings, no new signage, no text, no sun, no moon, no lens flare, no rain streaks crossing the lens. The exposure, white balance and colour grade are identical in every frame — deep blue-violet night, magenta and cyan neon.

The final frame matches the first exactly, so the clip loops without a visible cut.
